## Chapter 1 — Signing up

Several garages that buy from Mina mention that they've started using CarPal. She signs up on the desktop with her business email and a password, and confirms the email.

At **"How will you mainly use CarPal?"** she picks **Parts vendor**. The consent screen includes one vendor-specific item: **"Allow AI to work on your private inventory data"** to clean names, suggest categories and fitment, and find duplicates. The explanation states that her inventory data is used only for her shop, and never to help another vendor. She allows it.

**Behind the scenes**
- Screens: S-AUTH-03 Sign Up → email verification → S-AUTH-04 Role Selection → S-AUTH-05 Consent.
- `UserAccount` created; the vendor wizard creates the `Organization` (type `vendor`) and her owner `RoleMembership`.
- `ConsentRecord` type `vendor_inventory_ai_use` (organization subject). MCP rule: "AI cannot use one vendor's private inventory to benefit another vendor" (MVP §8.5).
- Event: `user.registered`.

**Other paths**
- *She also repairs cars:* she could register a hybrid provider+vendor organization instead; the vendor module is enabled separately (Doc 56 §2.11).

## Chapter 2 — Building the shop profile and catalogue

**Shop basics.** Name: Mina Parts. Type: parts retailer. Description, phone, opening hours, address and pin on the map. Delivery options: pickup at the counter, same-day courier within the city.

**What she sells.** She chooses part categories (brake pads, discs, control arms, struts, bushings, filters, sensors, water pumps, lighting), brands carried (Lemförder, Meyle, Bosch, ATE, TRW, OEM), focus (new OEM and aftermarket, some used), and ticks **"We supply trade customers"**.

**Verification.** She uploads her business registration. The profile can be prepared while verification is pending.

**Inventory setup.** She chooses **Upload a file** and drops in her spreadsheet export.

1. **Map columns.** The wizard reads the header row and guesses the mapping: "Code" → SKU, "Desc" → name, "OEM No" → part number, "Qty" → stock, "Price" → public price, "Price T" → trade price. She corrects one: "Make" is the *part* brand, not the car brand.
2. **Preview and validation.** 3,412 rows. The preview reports 41 rows with no price, 18 duplicate SKUs, and 6 rows with text in the quantity column. She chooses to import rows with no price as **Price on request**, and to skip the 6 broken rows.
3. **AI suggestions.** Before confirming, she sees a sample of what the AI will propose: cleaned names, categories, and fitment.
4. **Import.** She confirms. A progress bar runs in the background; she keeps working and gets a notification 6 minutes later: **Import finished — 3,388 items imported, 24 skipped.** The report lists the skipped rows and why, and she can download it.

**AI normalisation queue.** The next screen shows suggestions in batches she can review quickly:

| Raw name | Suggested |
| :--- | :--- |
| `QUERLENKER VA RE E90 LEMF` | Control arm · Front lower · Right · Lemförder · Fits BMW 3 Series E90 (likely) |
| `brake pad set front VW Golf 7 MK7 2015-2018` | Brake pads · Front · Volkswagen Golf MK7 2015–2018 (likely) |
| `BOSCH O2 SENSOR 0258` (×2, different SKUs) | Possible duplicate — merge? |

She accepts most suggestions in bulk with **Apply to similar items**, edits a few, and rejects the duplicate merge because the two sensors have different cable lengths.

**Fitment.** In the **Fitment Editor**, every AI fitment suggestion is labelled **Likely** until she confirms it. She opens the Lemförder control arm, checks the OE part number against her supplier's catalogue, and confirms fitment for BMW 3 Series E90/E91/E92/E93, 2005–2012. It becomes **Confirmed**. For a batch of used parts with no part numbers, the AI marks fitment as **Uncertain** and asks her to check; she leaves those for later.

**Prices.** For **price visibility** she chooses: public price shown for new parts; trade price visible only to approved trade customers; used parts "price on request".

She publishes. The dashboard shows **Catalogue quality: 61/100** with the top fixes: 1,240 items without images, 310 items without fitment, 95 items without warranty information.

**Behind the scenes**
- Screens: V-ONB-01 Vendor Basics, V-ONB-02 Categories and Brands, V-ONB-03 Inventory Setup, V-INV-04 Bulk Import Wizard, V-INV-05 AI Normalization Queue, V-INV-06 Fitment Editor, V-DASH-01.
- `VendorProfile`, `VendorCoverage`, `OrganizationLocation`, `OrganizationVerification` (admin review in A-VER-01).
- `InventoryImportBatch` runs as a background job (BullMQ). The UI is never blocked; a 10k-row import must run with a progress and error report (WP7 acceptance). CSV vendor import is a Should-have; manual creation is the Must-have fallback.
- `InventoryItem` rows with `trade_price` stored but never returned to public users (Module D security, WP7 price visibility matrix).
- AI normalisation (`normalize_inventory_item`, `detect_duplicate_inventory`) and fitment (`suggest_fitment`) run through MCP with her `vendor_inventory_ai_use` consent. Each suggestion is an `AiSuggestion`; nothing changes until she accepts.
- `InventoryFitment`: AI suggestions start as `likely` and `pending_vendor_confirmation`. Only her confirmation sets `confirmed` with `confirmed_by` (Doc 52).
- Catalogue quality score per Doc 54 §7.2.
- Events: `vendor.inventory.imported`, `inventory.updated` → search indexer; notification "inventory import finished" (Module L).

**Other paths**
- *Wrong file type or empty file:* "File invalid" with an example template to download.
- *Required columns not mapped:* "Mapping incomplete"; import can't start.
- *Plan limit:* if the import would exceed her plan's inventory item limit, the wizard says how many items fit and offers the Pro trial (WP3 entitlements).
- *No file at all:* she adds items one by one (V-INV-03) or starts with sample items.

## Chapter 3 — Daily login

Each morning Mina opens the vendor portal on the counter computer. She stays logged in on that machine; on her phone she logs in with an email code when she needs to.

The **Dashboard** shows open inquiries, catalogue quality, recent reviews, AI suggestions waiting for review, and **Popular searches near you** ("E90 control arm", "Golf 7 brake pads", "Peugeot 206 water pump").

She invites Kaveh as a team member with access to **Inventory** and **Inquiries**, but not to prices, reviews or settings.

**Behind the scenes**
- Screens: S-AUTH-02 Login, V-DASH-01 Vendor Dashboard, team management (vendor equivalent of P-TEAM-01).
- Vendor staff roles come from the WP0 permission matrix. Kaveh's actions are recorded with his own user ID.
- Popular searches are aggregated from search events (vendor insights, WP16). They never show who searched.

## Chapter 4 — Keeping the catalogue honest

Mina learns quickly that CarPal rewards an accurate catalogue, so keeping it up to date becomes routine.

- **Stock.** Kaveh updates stock in bulk every evening from the counter screen. Items at 0 show **Out of stock**; items under 3 show **Low stock**. Once a week Mina re-imports her accounting export to correct quantities; the importer matches existing SKUs instead of creating duplicates.
- **New items.** A delivery of Meyle sway bar links arrives. Kaveh adds them by hand with a photo from his phone. The AI suggests the category and likely fitment from the part number; Mina confirms the fitment later.
- **Data quality alerts.** A notification: *"42 items have no images and are among your most viewed."* She photographs the top 20.
- **Discontinued items.** She marks 60 items she will no longer stock as **Discontinued**. They disappear from search but stay in her records.
- **Profile.** She adds Saturday afternoon hours and changes courier delivery to "same day if ordered before 14:00".
- **Trade customers.** Reza Auto Suspension, a garage she has supplied for years, sends a **trade access request** through CarPal. She approves it. Reza can now see her trade prices; nobody else can.

**Behind the scenes**
- Screens: V-INV-01 Inventory List, V-INV-02 Item Detail, V-INV-03 Add/Edit Item, V-INV-04 (re-import), V-PROF-01 Vendor Profile Editor.
- Stock status enum: `in_stock`, `low_stock`, `out_of_stock`, `available_on_request`. Inventory status `discontinued` removes the item from public search.
- `VendorTradeAccount` (vendor = Mina Parts, customer = Reza Auto Suspension) → `approved`, with `approved_by` and `approved_at`. Unique per vendor and customer (Doc 52).
- Tenant isolation: vendors only see their own inventory; public users see only public price and stock; anti-scraping rate limits (Module D, WP7). Search index documents never contain trade prices (WP8 acceptance).
- Event: `inventory.updated` → re-index.

**Other paths**
- *Unknown trade request:* she rejects it, optionally with a note. She can revoke an approved account at any time.
- *Admin flags an item* (e.g. a counterfeit concern or a fitment complaint): the item goes to `pending_review` and Mina is notified with the reason (A-INV-01 Inventory Moderation).

## Chapter 5 — Getting found

Mina never sees most of the people who find her, but this is how they do:

- A car owner searching **"brake pads Golf 7"** with her Golf selected in My Garage sees parts filtered to that car. Mina's pads appear with **Confirmed fit**, stock, distance and public price.
- A garage searching **"front lower control arm right"** from a customer's appointment sees only parts that fit that customer's car, filtered by distance and **in stock now**. Approved trade customers also see trade prices.
- Her public **vendor page** shows her categories, brands, delivery options, a **Fitment coverage** summary ("BMW 3 Series: 412 parts"), her ratings, and her **Trade / B2B** label.

Results rank by fitment confidence, stock, price, condition, vendor rating, distance and how complete the listing is. Items with confirmed fitment rank above "Likely" ones, which is why Mina spends time confirming fitment.

The **Insights** page shows her what people look for: most searched parts, searches with no matching stock nearby ("Mercedes W205 rear shock absorbers"), items with missing fitment that are frequently viewed, and popular categories. She orders a small batch of W205 shocks.

**Behind the scenes**
- Screens (customer side): C-SEARCH-01, C-INV-01, C-INV-02, C-VEND-01. Vendor side: V-INS-01 Vendor Insights.
- Inventory ranking factors per MVP §12. Contextual search applies the selected vehicle's brand, model, series, engine and year automatically.
- Inventory gap suggestions based on local demand are a Should-have (MVP §18). They come from aggregated, anonymised demand and never from another vendor's private catalogue (MVP §8.5).

## Chapter 6 — Reza's inquiry

On a Thursday afternoon, the dashboard shows a new inquiry and her phone buzzes: **New inquiry — Lemförder control arm, front lower right — Reza Auto Suspension.**

The inquiry detail shows:
- **Item:** Lemförder front lower control arm, right, OE number, 2 in stock, fitment **Confirmed** for E90 2005–2012.
- **Requester:** Reza Auto Suspension (garage, approved trade customer).
- **Vehicle context:** BMW 3 Series E90, 2012, petrol 2.0. No VIN, no plate, no customer name.
- **Message:** *"Need it tomorrow morning for a customer car. Can you hold one?"*
- **Quantity:** 1.

Kaveh checks the shelf: two in the box, undamaged. Mina taps **Reserve**, sets the hold until **Friday 12:00**, and replies: *"Held for you until 12 tomorrow. Trade price as usual."* The whole thing takes 20 minutes. The reserved unit now shows as reserved in her inventory, and the public stock count drops to 1.

That same afternoon two other inquiries arrive:
- A car owner asks for **front brake pads for a 2017 Golf 7**. The listed item is out of stock, so Mina taps **Suggest alternative item** and offers the ATE equivalent she does have.
- Someone asks for a **used Peugeot 206 headlamp**. She has none left and marks it **Unavailable**, which updates the item's stock to 0 at the same time.

**Behind the scenes**
- Screens: V-INQ-01 Inquiry List (open, answered, reserved, closed), V-INQ-02 Inquiry Detail (confirm available, reserve, mark unavailable, suggest alternative, send response).
- `StockInquiry` with `requester_type = provider`, `requester_organization_id`, `vehicle_context_snapshot` limited to brand, model, series, year, engine (Doc 52: no VIN or plate). `consent_reference_id` points to the requester's `vendor_fitment_context` consent. Vendors see vehicle context only for fitment inquiries (MVP §14 principle 3).
- Status `sent` → `reserved` with `reserved_until`; `first_response_at` stored for the response-time metric. Replies are `InquiryMessage`s (thread-ready for future live chat, Plan §8.4).
- No checkout or payment in MVP; reservation is the end of the online flow (MVP §5.9). The "reserved" status is the hook for future parts commerce (Plan §8.4).
- Events: `inquiry.sent` → vendor notification; the reply → requester notification ("vendor inquiry answered", Module L).

**Other paths**
- *She doesn't answer within the expected time:* the requester sees "Waiting for vendor", and slow responses lower her inquiry responsiveness score (Doc 54 §3.2).
- *Reservation expires:* status returns to answered, the item is released, and both sides are notified.
- *Fitment doubt:* she can reply asking for the part number from the old part before reserving.

## Chapter 7 — The handover

Friday at 9:00, Reza collects the control arm at the counter and pays as usual on his garage account, outside CarPal. Kaveh marks the inquiry **Completed** and reduces stock. The Golf pads inquiry is also completed later that day, when the owner picks up the ATE pads.

**Behind the scenes**
- `StockInquiry` → `completed` with `closed_at`. Stock quantity is updated on the item. Payment happens outside CarPal in MVP.
- Completed inquiries count toward `inquiry_fulfillment_rate` (Doc 54 §3.2) and make the requester eligible to review the vendor in that context.
- The part number and item can be linked into the repair's `parts_used` on the car's service record when the garage records it (Doc 52 `VehicleServiceRecord.parts_used.inventory_item_id`), which ties the part to the car's history without exposing anything private to the vendor.

**Other paths**
- *Buyer doesn't come:* Mina cancels the reservation; status `cancelled`. Nothing is counted against her.
- *Wrong part:* if the part doesn't fit, the buyer can report it. A fitment complaint on a "Confirmed" item goes to inventory moderation, and the fitment can be downgraded.

## Chapter 8 — Recording the experience

**Reviews.** A few days later, Mina receives a notification: **New review — 5 stars from Reza Auto Suspension.** It rates part accuracy, availability accuracy, price fairness, service speed and communication, with the note *"Exactly the part listed, held it overnight, ready when I arrived."* It is marked as linked to a completed inquiry. She writes a short public thank-you.

Two weeks later a car owner leaves 3 stars: *"Website said in stock, but when I called they'd sold the last one."* The review is about availability accuracy. Mina replies politely, explaining that stock is now updated every evening, and she moves her weekly re-import to twice a week.

**Her numbers.** After three months, the dashboard shows:
- **Trust score** made of review quality, inquiry responsiveness (median first response 25 minutes), catalogue quality (now 84/100) and profile integrity.
- **Inventory analytics:** 3,350 indexed items, search appearances per week, inquiries received, inquiry-to-response time, and fitment completion (from 64% to 91% of items).
- **Demand insights:** top searched parts that she doesn't stock yet.

The search ranking change is visible too: her confirmed-fitment items appear near the top for E90 and Golf searches in her area, and the garages that use CarPal send her more inquiries than phone calls.

**Behind the scenes**
- Screens: V-REV-01 Vendor Reviews (ratings, response tools), V-INS-01 Vendor Insights, V-DASH-01.
- `Review` with `subject_type = vendor`, `context_stock_inquiry_id`, vendor sub-ratings (`part_accuracy`, `availability_accuracy`, `price_fairness`, `service_speed`, `communication`). One review per user per inquiry context; users cannot review an organization they belong to (Doc 52).
- Vendor trust score: review quality 40%, inquiry responsiveness 25%, catalogue quality 20%, profile integrity 15% (Doc 54 §3.2).
- Vendor value metrics: inventory items indexed, inventory search volume, inquiries received, inquiry-to-response time, fitment completion rate (MVP §16).
- Events: `review.published` → vendor notification ("review received", Module L), trust scoring.

**Other paths**
- *Fake or abusive review:* she reports it; a moderator decides, with an audit reason for any removal (WP15).
- *Catalogue quality drops* (many items without fitment or images after a big import): a "low data quality" alert and specific fixes on the dashboard.

## What this story proves (MVP §1)

| MVP claim | Where it shows in Mina's story |
| :--- | :--- |
| Trust can be structured | Fitment confidence labels, confirmed only by the vendor; vendor ratings tied to completed inquiries |
| Matching can be specific | Parts filtered by the exact vehicle; confirmed fitment ranks first |
| AI quietly improves every workflow | Column mapping, name normalisation, categories, fitment suggestions, duplicate detection, quality alerts |
| The platform can grow into an ecosystem | Stock inquiry and reservation are the hook for future checkout; parts link into vehicle service records |

Vendor inventory import completion rate · inventory items indexed · inventory search volume · inquiries received · inquiry-to-response time · fitment completion rate · AI suggestion acceptance rate.

## Open points found while writing this story

These are gaps or conflicts in the current docs. They belong to WP0.

1. **Trade access screens.** `VendorTradeAccount` exists in Doc 52, but Doc 90 has no screen for a vendor to see, approve, reject or revoke trade requests.
2. **AI reply drafts for vendors.** The AI review response assistant is defined for providers (MVP §7.3C). V-REV-01 lists "response tools" without saying whether AI drafts apply to vendors.
3. **Reserved stock.** Doc 52 has no field for reserved quantity. Does a reservation lower the public stock count automatically, as this story assumes?
4. **Inquiry completion.** The docs don't say who marks an inquiry `completed` (vendor, requester, or either), or whether completion is required before the requester can leave a verified vendor review. There is no verified-status value for inquiry-based vendor reviews in the Review verified status enum.
5. **Re-import behaviour.** The import wizard (V-INV-04) does not define how a repeat import updates existing SKUs versus creating new items, or how removed rows are handled.
6. **Vendor team roles.** As for providers, the WP0 permission matrix must define vendor staff roles (this story assumes owner and inventory/inquiries staff), including whether staff can see trade prices.
