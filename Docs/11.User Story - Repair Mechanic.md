## Chapter 1 — Signing up

A CarPal launch team member visits Reza's garage during the local launch and shows him how the platform matches customers by vehicle and problem. That evening Reza signs up on his phone with his mobile number and a password, and verifies the number with an SMS code.

At **"How will you mainly use CarPal?"** he picks **Service provider**. The consent screen is shorter than a car owner's. The one new item is **"Allow AI to analyse your business data to give you insights"** (skill heatmap, improvement suggestions). It explains that this uses only his own shop's data and that the results are visible only to his team. He allows it.

**Behind the scenes**
- Screens: S-AUTH-03 Sign Up → verification → S-AUTH-04 Role Selection → S-AUTH-05 Consent.
- `UserAccount` created. No organization exists yet; the provider onboarding wizard creates it (Module A Provider onboarding).
- `ConsentRecord` type `provider_analytics_ai_use` is requested once the organization exists, with `subject_type = organization`. The skill heatmap requires it (Doc 54 §2.3).
- Event: `user.registered`.

**Other paths**
- *He already has a CarPal account as a car owner:* he logs in and chooses **Create a business**. The same account gets a new `RoleMembership` (owner) in the new organization (MVP §5.1 "John is an individual car owner. John also works at Mike's Shop.").
- *Concierge onboarding:* a CarPal staff member can help set up the profile, but the business owner must accept the terms and own the account (MVP §20 cold start).

## Chapter 2 — Building the business profile

The onboarding wizard has eight short steps with a progress bar and a **Save and continue later** link on every step.

1. **Business basics.** Name: Reza Auto Suspension. Type: independent mechanic / garage. A two-line description. Phone and WhatsApp-style contact preference. Languages: Persian, English. Price tier: mid-range. He taps **Help me write this**, and the AI drafts a cleaner description from his notes. He shortens it and keeps it.
2. **Location and service area.** He drops a pin on the garage, adds access notes ("entrance from the side street"), and confirms the address. He doesn't offer mobile service.
3. **Services and capabilities.** He picks service systems as chips: Suspension/steering, Brakes, Wheels/tyres (alignment). Then specific tasks: control arm replacement, shock/strut replacement, bushing replacement, wheel alignment, steering rack repair, brake pad replacement. For each he can set an experience level; he marks suspension tasks "Advanced".
4. **Brands, models, series.** He selects BMW → 3 Series → E90, F30; BMW → 5 Series → E60, F10; Mercedes-Benz → C-Class → W204, W205. The AI suggests adding E91/E92 (the touring and coupé versions of the E90, which share suspension parts). He accepts E91 and E92.
5. **Portfolio upload.** He uploads 14 photos from his phone gallery. The AI groups them into 5 jobs, pairs before/after shots, suggests captions and tags, and flags 3 photos where a number plate is visible. He accepts the blurring. For each job, he must confirm whether the customer agreed to publication. He has permission for 2 jobs, so only those 2 are set to be public; the other 3 stay private evidence.
6. **Hours and appointment capacity.** Saturday to Wednesday 8:00–17:00, Thursday 8:00–13:00. Appointment types: diagnosis (60 min), repair (from 2 h), inspection (45 min). 30 minutes buffer between jobs. Max 2 jobs at the same time (two lifts).
7. **Verification.** He confirms the business phone with a call-back code and uploads his trade licence. A notice says a CarPal admin will review it within 2 working days, and that until then his profile shows "Verification pending".
8. **AI profile preview.** The wizard shows the public profile as customers will see it, and a completeness score of 72%. The AI lists suggestions:
   - *"Your photos show W205 C-Class front suspension work. Add 'Mercedes C-Class W205 — Suspension' as a capability?"* — he accepts.
   - *"You listed 'steering rack repair' but have no evidence for it yet. It will appear as 'Offered' until there are jobs or reviews."* — he keeps it.
   - *"Add a manufacturer or industry certification to raise your profile."* — later.

He taps **Publish**. The profile is live with the label **Verification pending**. Every capability shows as **Offered** because he has no verified evidence yet; his photos count as portfolio evidence only for the 2 consented jobs.

Two days later he receives a notification: **Your business is verified.** The "Verified Business" badge appears.

**Behind the scenes**
- Screens: P-ONB-01 Business Basics → P-ONB-02 Locations → P-ONB-03 Services & Capabilities → P-ONB-04 Brands/Models/Series → P-ONB-05 Portfolio Upload → P-ONB-06 Hours & Capacity → P-ONB-07 Verification → P-ONB-08 AI Profile Preview. Admin side: A-VER-01 Verification Queue.
- `Organization` (type `provider`), `ProviderProfile`, `OrganizationLocation` with geo point, `ProviderServiceCategory`, `ProviderVehicleCoverage`, `ProviderCapability` rows with `source = self_declared` → confidence level 0, public label "Offered" (Doc 54 §2.1).
- AI-suggested capability (W205) is created with `source = ai_suggested`, `status = pending_provider_approval`, and an `AiInsight` that cites the evidence. It becomes `active` only on his approval (Doc 54 §2.2, MVP "AI suggests, provider approves, platform verifies").
- `PortfolioItem`s and `MediaAsset`s: EXIF stripped, malware scanned, redaction applied before publishing. Public only with consent `granted` or `not_required` (WP13, Doc 52 media rules).
- `ProviderAvailabilityRule` rows for hours, buffers and capacity.
- `OrganizationVerification` → `pending` → `verified` by admin, with audit (WP15). Publish conditions: name, one active location, one service category, one supported brand (Doc 56 §2.10).
- Profile completeness follows the Module C criteria (business info, location verified, hours, categories, portfolio, reviews, response rate, availability enabled, certifications).
- Events: `provider.profile.published` → search indexer; `portfolio.published`; `ai.insight.generated`.

**Other paths**
- *Verification rejected* (licence unreadable): status `rejected` with a reason, and a request to upload again. The profile stays public without the badge.
- *Mobile mechanic:* a service area replaces the physical address (Doc 56 §2.11).
- *He tries to claim 40 brands with no evidence:* allowed (unlimited combinations, Module C), but every unbacked claim stays "Offered" and the AI warns about weak claims. Search ranks on evidence, not on claims.

## Chapter 3 — Daily login

Reza logs in each morning on his phone with a one-time code. Neda logs in on the office desktop with her own account; Reza invited her (Chapter 4).

Reza also owns a family car, so he added it as a car owner too. The top bar now has a switcher: **Reza (personal)** and **Reza Auto Suspension**. He switches to the shop.

The **Dashboard** shows today's appointments, new requests waiting for an answer, new reviews, AI suggestions, his profile completeness, and his average response time to requests. In the first weeks it also shows the onboarding checklist: add certification, publish first post, enable appointment requests.

**Behind the scenes**
- Screens: S-AUTH-02 Login, S-SHARED-06 Organization Switcher, P-DASH-01 Provider Dashboard.
- One user, two contexts: individual owner and provider owner (`RoleMembership`). The tenancy context changes with the switch; RLS ensures shop data is never visible in his personal context and vice versa (Plan §5 rule 1).
- Response time shown on the dashboard is the same `avg_response_time` signal used in the trust score (Doc 54 §3.1).

**Other paths**
- *Neda tries to open Insights:* her role doesn't include it, so she sees the "No permission" screen (S-SHARED-07).

## Chapter 4 — Growing and correcting the profile

Over the first months, Reza keeps improving the profile, often prompted by the platform.

- **Team.** He invites Neda as *Bookings* (requests, calendar, customer notes) and Hamid as *Technician* (assigned jobs and status updates only).
- **Certification.** He uploads a suspension systems course certificate from a recognised training body. After admin review it shows on the profile and adds strong evidence to his suspension capabilities.
- **Time off.** He blocks the first week of the new year in **Availability Settings**. Requests for those days are not offered.
- **Past customers.** CarPal invites him to bring past customers onto the platform. He sends review invitations to 12 recent customers from his own records. Customers who join and confirm the job create verified records for him.
- **AI suggestions.** Every couple of weeks an **AI Profile Suggestions** card appears:
  - *"8 of your recent jobs were front control arms on BMW F30. Add 'BMW 3 Series F30 — Control arm replacement' as a highlighted service?"* — Accept.
  - *"Your profile lists 'Steering rack repair' but you have had no requests or evidence for it in 3 months. Consider removing it."* — Reject. He does the work; he just hasn't had requests on CarPal yet.
  - *"Your description says 'all European cars'. Your evidence is almost entirely BMW and Mercedes. Consider being specific."* — He edits the description.
- **First badge.** By the third month, completed CarPal appointments and verified reviews for BMW 3 Series suspension work push that capability past the evidence threshold. A notification tells him he is now shown as **Suspension Specialist** for BMW 3 Series, with a link to the evidence behind it.
- **Trial.** He gets a 60-day Provider Pro trial, which unlocks the full insights dashboard and more AI drafts. A banner shows how many days are left.
- **Preview.** Before publishing changes, he checks **Public Profile Preview** to see exactly what customers see.

**Behind the scenes**
- Screens: P-TEAM-01 Team Management, P-PROF-01 to P-PROF-04 editors, P-PROF-05 Public Profile Preview, P-CAL-02 Availability Settings, P-INS-04 AI Profile Suggestions, P-SET-01 Provider Settings.
- Team users are `RoleMembership`s with permissions from the WP0 permission matrix.
- `ProviderCertification` → admin review → `verified`. A verified certification of type industry/manufacturer adds evidence weight 15; a trade licence only counts toward profile integrity (Doc 54 §1.1).
- Review invitations are `ReviewRequest`s (Should-have, WP9). Reviews coming from them are verified only if they meet a verified-status rule (Doc 52 §21); otherwise they are labelled unverified.
- Every AI suggestion is an `AiSuggestion` with accept/reject recorded in the evaluation store (AI suggestion acceptance rate, MVP §16).
- `ProviderTimeOff` blocks availability; trial is a plan flag with expiry (WP3).
- Event: `provider.profile.published` on each published change → search re-index.

**Other paths**
- *Trial expires:* Pro-only features become read-only; nothing public changes and no data is lost.
- *He accepts an AI suggestion that later turns out wrong:* he removes the capability; evidence links are kept for audit.

## Chapter 5 — Sara's request

Four months after joining, on a Tuesday evening, Reza's phone buzzes: **New appointment request — BMW 320i (E90), front suspension noise, within 2 weeks.**

Neda opens it the next morning. The request detail shows:
- **Customer:** Sara M., first visit.
- **Vehicle:** 2012 BMW 320i E90, 151,200 km.
- **AI pre-visit summary:** *"Customer reports a single knock from the front right over bumps, started two weeks ago and getting louder. No steering play, no pulling. Previous front brake pads and discs 14 months ago. Likely inspection areas: front control arm bushings, sway bar links, strut mounts."*
- **Video:** 10 seconds, the knock is clearly audible.
- **History:** the brake job, shared by the customer for this appointment.
- **Preferred times:** Thursday morning or Saturday morning.

The AI suggests a 2-hour repair slot and warns that a control arm on this model is often not in stock locally. Thursday morning is free, but if a part is needed the car would sit on a lift all afternoon and block another booking. Neda proposes **Thursday 14:00** instead, with a note: *"We'll inspect first. An afternoon slot lets us order a part for the next morning if needed."*

**Behind the scenes**
- Screens: P-REQ-01 Appointment Request List → P-REQ-02 Appointment Request Detail; P-CAL-01 Calendar View.
- The `PreVisitReport` contains vehicle identity, mileage, symptoms, AI triage summary, likely categories, relevant history, photos/video, urgency and preferred contact method (Module H). History is shown only because Sara granted `service_history_sharing` for this appointment; without it, that section reads "Not shared by customer" (WP10 acceptance).
- AI appointment suggestions (`suggest_appointment_slot`, MVP §7.3F): best slot, conflicts, longer duration for complex jobs. They are suggestions; Neda decides.
- Status: `requested` → `provider_reviewing` (opened) → `alternative_proposed`. Response time is measured from `requested` to the first response.
- Customer notes: Neda adds a private `ProviderCustomerNote` ("prefers updates by app message"). Org-private.

**Other paths**
- *Decline:* requires a reason; the customer is shown other providers (Consumer story, Chapter 8).
- *Ask for more information:* a message back to the customer, status stays `provider_reviewing`.
- *Safety-critical request:* the request detail shows a red banner, and the dashboard lists it first.
- *Request for a car the shop doesn't cover:* the AI notes the mismatch ("No evidence for this brand in your profile"); Neda can still accept.

## Chapter 6 — The part

Sara accepts Thursday 14:00. On Thursday at 14:30, with Silver on the lift, Reza confirms it: the front right lower control arm's rear bushing is torn. He moves the appointment to **In progress** and then looks for a part.

He searches CarPal parts from the appointment: **"Front lower control arm, right"**. The search already knows the vehicle (BMW 3 Series E90, 2012, petrol) from the appointment and shows only parts with matching fitment. He filters to **in stock** and **within 15 km**.

The top result is from **Mina Parts** (a vendor in [Story 12](12.User%20Story%20-%20Parts%20Vendor.md)): a Lemförder control arm, OE part number listed, fitment **Confirmed** for E90 2005–2012, 2 in stock, 6 km away. Because Reza's shop has an approved trade account with Mina Parts, he sees the trade price as well as the public price.

He taps **Ask about this part**, sets quantity 1, and writes: *"Need it tomorrow morning for a customer car. Can you hold one?"* The inquiry includes the vehicle's brand, model, series, year and engine, but not Sara's VIN or plate.

Twenty minutes later: **Mina Parts reserved 1 item for you until tomorrow 12:00.** Reza sets Sara's appointment to **Awaiting parts**, with the note: *"Lower control arm bushing torn. Replacement part reserved, fitting tomorrow morning. Estimated completion Friday 13:00."* and the expected cost range.

**Behind the scenes**
- Screens: C-INV-01 Inventory Search Results, C-INV-02 Inventory Item Detail (used here in the provider context).
- Provider part search uses customer vehicle context, part number, category, vendor location, availability now and trade price (Module D "For provider"). Results rank by fitment confidence, stock, price, condition, vendor rating, distance, completeness (MVP §12).
- Trade price is visible only because a `VendorTradeAccount` (vendor = Mina Parts, customer = Reza Auto Suspension) has status `approved`. Public users never see it (Module D security).
- `StockInquiry` with `requester_type = provider`, `requester_organization_id`, `vehicle_context_snapshot` (brand, model, series, year, engine — no VIN or plate). Attaching vehicle context needs `vendor_fitment_context` consent. Status `sent` → `reserved` with `reserved_until`.
- Event: `inquiry.sent` → vendor notification (Module L "new inquiry").
- Appointment status `in_progress` → `awaiting_parts`; the customer is notified.

**Other paths**
- *No trade account yet:* he sees public prices only and a **Request trade access** option. The vendor approves or rejects (Story 12, Chapter 5).
- *Part unavailable:* the vendor marks the inquiry `unavailable` or suggests an alternative item. Reza tries the next vendor.
- *Fitment shown as "Likely" or "Uncertain":* Reza checks the part number before relying on it. Unconfirmed fitment is labelled, never shown as confirmed.

## Chapter 7 — The repair

Friday 9:00, Reza collects the part from Mina Parts. Hamid fits it and moves the job **In progress** from his phone. Reza test-drives the car and does the alignment check. At 12:20 Neda sets **Ready for pickup**.

When Sara collects the car, Neda closes the appointment as **Completed** and fills in the outcome: service system *Suspension*, task *Control arm replacement*, part used (Lemförder control arm, part number, quantity 1), labour and parts cost, warranty note "12 months on part and labour". She attaches a photo of the invoice and adds an internal note: *"Left side bushing starting to crack. Advised customer to check at next service."*

**Behind the scenes**
- Status history: `awaiting_parts` → `in_progress` → `ready_for_pickup` → `completed`. Completion needs an outcome or service record (Doc 52 Appointment rules).
- Completion creates a `VehicleServiceRecord` on Sara's car (`source_type = appointment_completed`, `verification_status = appointment_verified`, `parts_used` with the part number, cost breakdown) and a `ProviderJobLog` (`verification_status = appointment_verified`) linked to the appointment.
- Evidence: a `ProviderCapabilityEvidence` row of type `completed_appointment` (base weight 10) is added to *BMW / 3 Series / E90 / Suspension / Control arm replacement*. The same job is not counted again through the job log or service record (Doc 54 §1.1 no double counting).
- Events: `appointment.completed` → review request to the customer, service record creation, trust scoring, skill heatmap pipelines.

**Other paths**
- *Walk-in job, not booked on CarPal:* Reza logs it as a job log with status `self_reported`. It counts as evidence only after verification (e.g. the customer confirms, or an invoice is verified).
- *The customer doesn't show for pickup:* status stays `ready_for_pickup`; no effect on his scores.

## Chapter 8 — Recording the experience

**The review.** On Saturday, the day after the repair, a notification arrives: **New review — 5 stars, verified visit.** In the **Review Inbox** Reza reads Sara's review. The AI has already prepared a reply:

> *"Thank you, Sara, for trusting us with your E90. Glad the knock is gone. As mentioned, the left side should be checked at your next service. See you then."*

He changes "See you then" to "We're here whenever you need us" and publishes. Internally the reply is recorded as AI-assisted and edited.

**The showcase.** On Monday Reza opens **Portfolio → Create showcase** and uploads 4 photos from the job. The AI composer pairs the old and new arm, flags the plate visible in one shot, and drafts:

> *"Front right lower control arm replacement on a BMW 3 Series E90. Torn rear bushing caused a knock over bumps. Replaced with an OE-quality arm, alignment checked, completed the next morning."*

Tags: BMW, 3 Series, E90, Suspension, Control arm replacement. Reza removes "OE-quality" (he'd rather name the brand) and sends the customer a request to approve publishing. Sara approves that evening, and the showcase goes live on his profile and in the Discover feed for E90 owners nearby.

**A harder review.** The same week, a 2-star verified review arrives from another customer: *"Good repair but I waited 50 minutes past my appointment time and nobody told me."* The AI flags it as a timeliness complaint and drafts a reply that acknowledges the wait, apologises, explains nothing about the customer's car, and invites a private follow-up. It notes: *"No liability or legal sensitivity detected."* Reza edits it to mention the change he is making and publishes.

**What the platform tells him.** The **Insights** page updates over the following days:
- **Skill heatmap:** *Strong — BMW 3 Series E90/F30 suspension (4.9 rating, rising).* *Emerging — Mercedes C-Class W205 suspension.* *Opportunity — "High local demand for BMW 5 Series F10 suspension; your profile has little evidence for it."*
- **Capability levels:** Sara's job adds one more counted job to his BMW 3 Series suspension work, which already carries the Specialist badge (Chapter 4). His Mercedes C-Class W205 suspension capability moves from "Offered" to **Experienced** after its third verified job.
- **Improvement plan:** *"Two of your last six reviews mention waiting past the appointment time. Consider increasing the buffer between jobs from 30 to 45 minutes."* He changes the buffer in Availability Settings.

**The vendor.** CarPal also asks Reza to rate his inquiry with Mina Parts. He gives 5 stars, rating part accuracy, availability accuracy, price fairness, service speed and communication.

**Behind the scenes**
- Screens: P-PORT-02 Create/Edit Showcase, P-PORT-03 Media Library, P-REV-01 Review Inbox, P-REV-02 Review Detail & Response, P-REV-03 Review Analytics, P-INS-01 Skill Heatmap, P-INS-02 Capability Detail, P-INS-03 Improvement Plan.
- Showcase: AI composer output (`generate_showcase_caption`) is a draft; the `PortfolioItem` becomes public only after provider approval **and** customer `showcase_publishing` consent (MVP §7.3E, WP12).
- Review response: `ReviewResponse` with `ai_assisted = true`, `ai_draft_insight_id`, `edited_after_ai = true`, status `draft` → `published` by a human action only. AI must not admit liability, invent facts, disclose private data or use an aggressive tone (MVP §7.3C).
- Skill heatmap classifications follow Doc 54 §2.3 (strong: level 3+ and rating ≥ 4.5; opportunity: local demand ≥ 60 and level ≤ 1). Numbers come from the scoring protocol; only the explanation text is AI-written.
- Badge award is rule-based and auditable: capability `badge_eligible` at level 3 (`strong_evidence`, score 80–199), and every award has a stored rule evaluation (WP9 acceptance).
- Vendor review: `Review` with `subject_type = vendor`, `context_stock_inquiry_id`, vendor sub-rating set. Reza writes it as a user acting for his organization.
- Events: `review.published`, `portfolio.published`, `ai.insight.generated`.

**Other paths**
- *Abusive or fake review:* Reza flags it and can request mediation. A moderator decides; removal requires an audit reason. If upheld, its evidence is revoked and scores recalculated (Doc 54 §1.1).
- *He ignores the AI draft:* he writes his own reply or none. Response rate to reviews is a trust signal, so the dashboard reminds him of unanswered reviews.
- *Customer declines showcase consent:* the job remains private evidence; only the photos stay unpublished.
- *His rating drops in a category:* that capability can fall to "weak" in the heatmap, with suggestions; nothing is hidden from customers.

## Open points found while writing this story

These are gaps or conflicts in the current docs. They belong to WP0.

1. **No job logging screen.** `ProviderJobLog` and "Job logging" are MVP features (Module C), but Doc 90 has no provider screen for logging a walk-in job or for "basic service history entry for customers".
2. **No provider parts search screen.** The provider portal has no parts search screen. This story uses C-INV-01/C-INV-02 in provider context. How the appointment's vehicle context and trade pricing are passed into search should be specified.
3. **No trade account screens.** `VendorTradeAccount` exists (Doc 52), but no screen covers requesting trade access (provider side) or approving it (vendor side).
4. **Team roles for providers.** The permission matrix (WP0) must define provider staff roles. This story assumes owner, bookings and technician.
5. **Publish vs. verification.** Doc 56 §2.10 requires "business status is active" to publish, but §2.11 allows publishing with "Verification pending". This story follows §2.11. The rule should be made explicit.
6. **Showcase consent delivery.** How the provider sends the consent request to the customer, and what happens when the customer is not on CarPal, is not defined.
7. **AI disclosure on replies.** `ReviewResponse.ai_assisted` is stored, but the docs don't say whether it is shown publicly.
