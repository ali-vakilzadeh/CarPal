## Chapter 1 — Signing up

Sara hears about CarPal from a colleague whose Peugeot was finally fixed "by someone who'd seen the problem fifty times before". She installs the app. The splash screen shows the CarPal promise: *Find the right trusted expert for your exact vehicle and problem — not just the nearest shop.* The app opens in Persian, right-to-left, because her phone is set to Persian.

She taps **Sign up** and enters her name, mobile number, a password, and her country. She ticks the required boxes for terms and privacy. Two other boxes are optional and unticked: marketing messages and AI personalization. The screen explains in one sentence what AI personalization means ("CarPal can use your vehicles and history to tailor suggestions"). She ticks AI personalization but leaves marketing off.

A one-time code arrives by SMS. She enters it and her account is active.

Next, the app asks how she will mainly use CarPal. She picks **"I own or manage personal vehicles."** The other choices (fleet manager, service provider, parts vendor) are there too; the app notes that she can join a business later without a new account.

Last comes the consent screen. It explains each permission in plain language: location (to find nearby help), notifications (appointment updates and reminders), photos (to attach pictures of problems), and review invitations (CarPal may ask her to review a shop after a visit, in the app or by email with a personal link that works for 7 days). She allows location *while using the app*, allows notifications, and accepts review invitations.

**Behind the scenes**
- Screens: S-AUTH-01 Splash → S-AUTH-03 Sign Up → phone verification → S-AUTH-04 Role Selection → S-AUTH-05 Consent and Permissions.
- `UserAccount` created with status `pending_verification`, moving to `active` after the code is verified. Verified contact is required later to write reviews (Doc 52, Review rules).
- One `ConsentRecord` per choice, each with `policy_version`: `ai_personalization` (granted), `marketing` (not granted), `location_usage`, `review_invitations`. Phone stored in E.164; UI language FA with RTL layout and Persian digits (Plan §3).
- Role choice stored as a primary intent, not a lock-in (S-AUTH-04 "Important behavior"). `OnboardingProgress` starts for the individual flow.
- Event: `user.registered` → analytics (activation funnel, MVP §16), notification service (welcome message).

**Other paths**
- *Number already registered:* "Duplicate account" state offers login or password reset instead.
- *Declines location:* the app explains it will ask for a city or area when searching instead of using GPS. Nothing is blocked.
- *Declines AI personalization:* Help Me still works, but uses only what she types in that session, not her saved history (MVP §7.1 privacy minimization). She can turn it on later in AI Preferences.
- *Signs up with email instead of phone:* same flow with an email link or code.
- *Signs up with a phone number only:* review invitations arrive in the app. The app asks for an email address the first time an email invitation would help.

## Chapter 2 — Her first vehicle

Onboarding continues straight into **Add your first vehicle**. Sara has the registration card in her bag, so she types the VIN. The app decodes it and fills in BMW, 3 Series, E90, 2012, petrol, automatic. The model shows as "320i". Each field has a small edit icon; nothing the decoder filled is locked.

The app suggests she add the current mileage. She types 148,000 km and gives the car a nickname, "Silver". She adds a photo from her gallery. A completeness bar shows 70% and lists what is missing: colour, documents, service history. She skips these for now.

The next screen asks, **"Is anything wrong with this car right now?"** Nothing is, today. She taps **Not now** and lands on **Discover**. The home screen already knows about Silver: it shows "Recommended for your BMW 3 Series" with three nearby providers that have evidence of work on E90s, a seasonal tip about checking coolant before summer, and a large **Help Me** button. Beside the search bar is a **Browse on map** button, which she does not need yet.

**Behind the scenes**
- Screens: C-ONB-01 Add First Vehicle → C-ONB-02 First Problem (Optional) → C-HOME-01 Home / Discover.
- `Vehicle` created with `owner_type = user`, status `active`, `mileage_km`, profile completeness. VIN decode is a pluggable, optional service; manual entry always works (Plan §2, WP5).
- If the decoder is unavailable or the VIN is partial, the AI vehicle profile helper can suggest likely model and series. Suggestions are shown as suggestions, and she confirms them (MVP §7.2D).
- Plan check: Individual Free allows 5 active vehicles; this is vehicle 1 of 5 (MVP §3.1, WP3 entitlements enforced server-side).
- Event: `vehicle.created` → search context, maintenance reminder scheduling, analytics ("% of new users who add a vehicle").
- Home recommendations use contextual search filtered by her vehicle (MVP §12 Contextual search). Featured or paid placements, if any, are shown in a separate, labelled block and never mixed into the trusted list (MVP Rule 2).

**Other paths**
- *No VIN at hand:* she chooses brand → model → series → year from lists. Unknown series is allowed and flagged as a gap.
- *VIN and typed year conflict:* the helper warns "This VIN suggests 2011; you entered 2012" and lets her choose.
- *She does describe a problem at onboarding:* the flow jumps into Help Me (Chapter 6) with Silver already selected.

## Chapter 3 — Coming back

A week later Sara opens the app on her laptop through the web version. She enters her mobile number and chooses **Send me a code** instead of a password. The code arrives, and she is in. Her garage, preferences, and Persian layout are already there.

Later that month she gets a new phone. Logging in on the new device works the same way. The app asks again for notification permission on the new device, since that permission belongs to the device, not the account.

**Behind the scenes**
- Screen: S-AUTH-02 Login (password or one-time code, forgot password, optional MFA).
- Sessions are per device. `PushDeviceToken` is registered per device; consent records belong to the account and do not need to be given again.
- UI language follows her saved preference. The app uses its bundled UI dictionary if the CDN is unreachable (Plan §3.1, WP19 acceptance).

**Other paths**
- *Wrong code or password several times:* "Locked account" state with a timed lock and a recovery route.
- *MFA turned on:* an extra step after the password (optional in MVP, §9.3).
- *Account suspended by moderation:* login explains the suspension and how to appeal.

## Chapter 4 — Building out her profile

On a quiet Sunday, Sara sets things up properly.

**Personal profile.** In **Profile** she adds a profile photo and a display name ("Sara M."). The app explains that only her display name appears on public reviews; her phone number and full name are never shown.

**AI and privacy preferences.** In **AI Preferences** she sees that AI personalization is on, with a short list of what it uses: her vehicles, service history, and active issues. In **Privacy & Consent** she sees every consent she gave at signup, each with a date and a switch.

**The second car.** In **My Garage** she taps **Add vehicle** and adds the Corolla with manual entry. The garage now shows two cards, and a small line reads "2 of 5 vehicles on the free plan".

**Service history.** Back on Silver, she opens the **Service History** tab, which is empty. She taps **Add service record** and records the brake job from last spring at her old garage: date, "front brake pads and discs", the garage name, the mileage, and the cost. She photographs the paper invoice and attaches it. While she types, the app suggests the category *Brakes → Brake pad replacement*, and she accepts it. The record appears on the timeline, marked as added by her, with the invoice attached.

**Documents.** In the **Documents** tab she uploads her insurance certificate and sets the expiry date. The app offers a reminder two weeks before expiry, and she accepts.

Silver's completeness bar is now 95%.

**Behind the scenes**
- Screens: C-PROF-01 Profile Home, C-PROF-03 AI Preferences, C-PROF-02 Privacy & Consent, C-GARAGE-01 My Garage List, C-GARAGE-03 Add/Edit Vehicle, C-GARAGE-02 Vehicle Detail (Service History, Documents, Reminders tabs), C-GARAGE-05 Add Service Record.
- `VehicleServiceRecord` with `source_type = user_manual` (or `invoice_upload`), `created_by_type = user`, `visibility = private` by default. The invoice is a `ServiceRecordEvidence` of type `invoice` linked to a `MediaAsset` that is malware-scanned and EXIF-stripped (WP13).
- Verified and unverified records are visually separated on the timeline (WP5 acceptance).
- Category suggestion is an `AiInsight` (`extract service category from free text`, Module I). The record stores the category she confirmed, never the raw AI output (Plan §5 rule 6).
- `VehicleDocument` stored encrypted with access control (WP5). `MaintenanceReminder` created for the insurance expiry.
- Events: `vehicle.created`, `service_record.created`.

**Other paths**
- *Duplicate record:* if she enters the same brake job twice, the AI duplicate check warns her before saving (Module I).
- *Adding a sixth active vehicle:* blocked with C-PLAN-01 Vehicle Limit, which offers to mark a vehicle as sold or inactive. Sold vehicles keep their history (Module B statuses).

## Chapter 5 — Keeping it accurate

Over the next weeks Sara's details change, and she keeps the profile current.

- **Mileage.** When the app reminds her monthly, she updates Silver to 151,200 km from the vehicle card.
- **A wrong field.** She notices the decoder set Silver's colour to "grey". She edits it to "silver". The edit screen shows which fields came from the VIN decoder and which she entered herself.
- **Notifications.** Too many tips arrive by email, so in notification preferences she switches tips to in-app only. Appointment updates stay on push. The settings note that safety alerts are always delivered.
- **Consent.** She notices she never meant to allow review invitations by SMS, only in-app. She changes the channel. She also confirms that marketing is still off.
- **Language.** She switches the app to English for a day to show a friend, then back to Persian. Her own data (vehicle nickname, notes) stays as she typed it; platform labels (categories, symptoms) switch language.

**Behind the scenes**
- Screens: C-GARAGE-03 Add/Edit Vehicle, C-PROF-02 Privacy & Consent, S-SHARED-05 Profile/Account Menu (notification preferences).
- `VehicleMileageLog` keeps every mileage update for history and maintenance suggestions. Event `vehicle.updated`.
- Consent changes write a new state on `ConsentRecord` (`withdrawn_at` or a new grant). Withdrawal takes effect immediately and is audited (Plan §5 rules 2 and 8, WP2 acceptance).
- Critical alerts are never suppressed by marketing or frequency opt-outs (WP14 acceptance).
- Taxonomy labels come from JSONB translations with English fallback; user-entered text keeps its `content_locale` (Plan §3.3).

**Other paths**
- *Rectification of something she can't edit* (e.g. a provider-created record that names the wrong car): she reports it from the record, which opens a correction request (MVP §14 right to rectification).
- *She sells the Corolla:* status `sold`; it moves out of the active count, its history stays exportable.

## Chapter 6 — "Something is clunking"

Two months later, driving to work, Sara hears a dull *clunk* from the front right every time Silver goes over a speed bump. It doesn't feel dangerous, but it is getting louder.

That evening she taps **Help Me**. The app asks which vehicle; she picks Silver. She types, in Persian: *"A knocking sound from the front right when I go over bumps. Started two weeks ago, getting louder."* She also records a 10-second video with the phone held near the wheel while her husband rocks the car.

The assistant replies with three short questions, one at a time, with buttons:
1. *Does it happen when turning the steering wheel while parked?* — **No.**
2. *Do you feel anything in the steering wheel, like play or vibration?* — **No.**
3. *Is it a single knock or a rattle?* — **Single knock.**

Then a **safety check** card appears. Because the noise is from the suspension area, the app asks one more question: *Does the car pull to one side or does the steering feel loose?* She answers **No**. The card turns amber: **"Not urgent, but should be inspected within the next two weeks. If the steering starts to feel loose, stop driving and get it inspected."**

The summary screen reads:

> *"Based on your description and your vehicle, this is most likely related to the **front suspension** (for example: sway bar links, control arm bushings, or strut mounts). This is not a diagnosis; a mechanic needs to inspect the car. Your records show front brake work 14 months ago, which is unlikely to be related."*

It shows **Suspension → Suspension inspection/repair** as the likely service category, with a note "Confidence: medium". Sara can correct the category if she thinks it is wrong. She leaves it.

**Behind the scenes**
- Screens (Doc 55 triage wizard): C-HELP-01 Landing → C-HELP-02 Select Vehicle → C-HELP-03 Symptom Intake → C-HELP-04 AI Clarifying Questions → C-HELP-05 Safety and Urgency Check → C-HELP-06 Issue Summary and Recommendations.
- A `TriageSession` records the conversation. The AI reads only her vehicle summary, the relevant part of her service history, and active issues, and only because `ai_personalization` is granted. All reads go through MCP (`summarize_vehicle_profile`, `summarize_service_history`, `detect_urgency`) and are written to the MCP access log (Plan §5 rule 5, WP17).
- The result is a `VehicleIssue` with `source = ai_triage`, `urgency_level = medium`, `status = open`, and `ai_triage_insight_id` pointing to the `AiInsight`. The AI text is never stored as the issue's own text (Doc 52).
- Safety rules (MVP §7.2A, §13 AI safety): no absolute diagnosis, never tell the user to ignore a symptom, escalate brakes/steering/airbag/fire/overheating/unsafe no-start.
- The video is uploaded as a `MediaAsset` (private, EXIF stripped).
- If AI is unavailable, the wizard falls back to a manual category picker and plain search (Plan §5 rule 7).

**Other paths**
- *Safety-critical answer* (e.g. "the steering feels loose"): urgency `safety_critical`, a red warning advising her not to drive, urgent providers first, and towing options if available (MVP §7.2A Safety handling).
- *Vague description* ("weird noise"): the assistant asks more questions before suggesting a category ("missing information prompts", Module E).
- *She disagrees with the category:* she changes it; the correction is recorded for AI evaluation (triage correction rate, MVP §16).

## Chapter 7 — Choosing a specialist

Below the summary, the app shows **three recommended providers**, each with a short reason:

> **Reza Auto Suspension** — 2.4 km · ★ 4.8 in suspension repairs
> *"Recommended because this shop has 19 completed suspension jobs on BMW 3 Series E90/F30, a 4.8 average rating for suspension work, and an opening on Thursday morning."*
> Badges: Verified Business · Suspension Specialist

The second result is a large general garage with a higher overall rating but almost no BMW evidence; its explanation says so honestly ("strong general ratings; limited BMW suspension evidence"). The third is a mobile mechanic. A separate, clearly labelled "Featured" strip below the list shows a paid placement.

Sara opens Reza's profile. The **Services & Capabilities** tab shows "BMW 3 Series (E90) — Suspension — Specialist", backed by job and review counts. The **Portfolio** tab has a before/after of a worn control arm bushing on an E90, with the number plate blurred. In **Reviews** she filters to "Suspension" and reads the verified ones; one review mentions a long wait, and Reza's reply explains he has since added time between appointments. She likes that he answered.

She taps the heart to **save him as a favourite** and **follows** the shop.

She taps **Request appointment**. The draft is already filled in: Silver, the symptom summary, the video, the suggested category, urgency "within two weeks". She picks two preferred time windows: Thursday morning or Saturday morning. The draft then asks what to share with the shop:
- ☑ Vehicle details (required for the request)
- ☑ Service history relevant to this issue
- ☑ The video and photos

She leaves all three ticked and sends the request. The confirmation screen says the shop usually responds within about 2 hours and that she will get a notification.

**Behind the scenes**
- Screens: C-HELP-06 (recommendations) → C-PROV-01 Provider Profile → C-PROV-02 Showcase Detail → C-HELP-07 Appointment Request Draft → C-HELP-08 Request Confirmation.
- Matching combines explicit fit, evidence fit, category rating, recency, availability, location, trust signals and user preference (Module E). Every result carries stored structured reasons; AI writes the sentence, and a template sentence is used if AI is off (WP8).
- "Specialist" badge appears only because the capability reached level 3 (`strong_evidence`) by the scoring rules, not because the shop claimed it (Doc 54 §2, MVP Rule 1).
- Featured placement is separate and never changes organic order (MVP Rule 2).
- `Favorite` and `Follow` records created (Module J).
- `Appointment` created with status `requested`, `source = ai_match`, preferred `AppointmentTimeWindow`s, linked `VehicleIssue`. `ConsentRecord`s for `vehicle_data_sharing` and `service_history_sharing`, scoped to this appointment and granted to Reza's organization.
- A `PreVisitReport` is generated (`create_pre_visit_report` via MCP) and is visible to the provider only because consent exists (WP10 acceptance).
- Events: `appointment.requested` → provider notification (Module L "new appointment request"), analytics ("appointment request conversion from AI recommendation").

**Other paths**
- *She skips Help Me and searches directly* ("BMW suspension", in Persian or English), or browses the map: C-SEARCH-01 or S-SHARED-02 with the same contextual filtering and explanations. The request source becomes `direct_search`. Chapter 11 follows this path in full.
- *She unticks service history:* the request still goes; the pre-visit report omits history and says the customer chose not to share it.
- *No good match nearby:* the app widens the radius, offers mobile mechanics, and explains why results are limited.

## Chapter 8 — The appointment

An hour later a notification arrives: **Reza Auto Suspension proposed a different time — Thursday 14:00 instead of the morning.** The note says the shop wants to inspect first and keep the afternoon open in case a part is needed. Sara accepts the new time with one tap, and the appointment becomes **Confirmed**.

The appointment page shows the address with a **Directions** button (it opens the map app she chooses), the shop's preferred contact method, and a **pre-visit report** she can read herself: the summary the mechanic will see, her video, and the shared history.

On Wednesday evening she gets a reminder. On Thursday at 13:55 she taps **I've arrived** on the check-in link. During the afternoon the status updates as the work progresses:

1. **In progress** — "Inspection started."
2. **Awaiting parts** — "Front right lower control arm bushing is worn. Replacement control arm reserved at a parts supplier; available tomorrow morning. Estimated completion: Friday 13:00." The shop adds the expected cost range in the note.
3. Sara replies through the appointment page that this is fine.
4. **Ready for pickup** — Friday 12:20.
5. **Completed** — when she collects the car.

The clunk is gone on the drive home.

**Behind the scenes**
- Screens: C-APP-01 Appointment List, C-APP-02 Appointment Detail, C-APP-03 Pre-Visit Report View; S-SHARED-04 Notifications Center.
- Status history: `requested` → `provider_reviewing` → `alternative_proposed` → `confirmed` (on her acceptance) → `checked_in` → `in_progress` → `awaiting_parts` → `in_progress` → `ready_for_pickup` → `completed`. Illegal transitions are rejected; each change is stored in `AppointmentStatusHistory` (WP10, Doc 52 enum).
- Wait-time features in play: reminder, check-in link, status updates, parts availability note, estimated completion window (Module G).
- Vehicle status moves to `in_repair` while the car is at the shop and back to `active` on completion.
- **Directions** (C-APP-02) opens the Directions sheet S-SHARED-10. Sara picks an external map app and the app remembers the choice. Only the shop's coordinates are passed to it.
- Events: `appointment.confirmed`, `appointment.completed` → service record, review request, trust and heatmap pipelines.
- The part reservation itself is between Reza and the vendor (see [Story 12](12.User%20Story%20-%20Parts%20Vendor.md), Chapter 6). Sara sees only the status note, not the supplier's private prices.

**Other paths**
- *She can't make Thursday:* she proposes another time or cancels; cancellation reason is recorded.
- *She doesn't show up:* the shop marks `no_show`. This is tracked against her, never against the shop's trust score (Doc 54 §3.1).
- *The shop declines:* she is offered the next recommended providers with the same draft, so she doesn't start over.

## Chapter 9 — Recording the experience

**The service record.** When the appointment is completed, a new entry appears on Silver's timeline: *Front right lower control arm replaced — Reza Auto Suspension — 151,900 km*, with the part used and the cost. It carries a **Verified** mark because it comes from a completed appointment. Sara photographs the paper invoice the shop gave her and attaches it to the record. The open issue "Clunk from front right over bumps" is marked **Resolved** and linked to this repair. The app adds a soft suggestion: *"Control arms on the E90 often wear in pairs. Consider asking about the left side at your next service."*

**The review.** The next morning she gets an in-app invitation and an email with a personal link: *How was your visit to Reza Auto Suspension?* The link works for 7 days and only for her; she opens it, confirms with a one-time code, and lands on the review form. The review form already shows the context, "Verified visit · BMW 3 Series · Suspension". She gives:
- Overall: ★★★★★
- Work quality 5, Communication 5, Timeliness 4, Price accuracy 5, Cleanliness 4, Would return: Yes

She writes that he explained the problem with the old part in his hand, sent clear updates, and that the final price matched the range he gave. She types her plate number in the text by accident; the form warns her that the text contains what looks like a licence plate and offers to remove it. She removes it. The form suggests tags: "explained clearly", "fair price", "fixed first time". She keeps the first two and removes the third because it is too early to know.

She submits. The confirmation says the review will appear after a quick automatic check. It is published within minutes. Because she had a confirmed appointment, nobody has to approve it: not the shop, and not CarPal staff.

**The showcase request.** Two days later she gets another request: Reza would like to publish the before/after photos of her old and new control arm as a showcase, with the plate and any faces blurred. She views the photos and approves. She could have declined with no effect on her review or her record.

**The reply.** That weekend Reza publicly replies to her review, thanking her and suggesting the left-side check at her next service. Sara gets a notification and marks another customer's review on his profile as **helpful**.

**Behind the scenes**
- Screens: C-GARAGE-04 Service History Detail, C-REV-01 Write Review, C-REV-02 Review Submitted, S-SHARED-04 Notifications.
- `VehicleServiceRecord` with `source_type = appointment_completed`, `verification_status = appointment_verified`, `appointment_id`, `resolves_issue_id`; `VehicleIssue` → `resolved`. Next-maintenance suggestion is an `AiInsight` linked via `next_service_insight_id`.
- `Review` with `verified_status = appointment_verified`, `trust_weight = 1.0`, `counts_toward_core_score = true`, provider sub-rating set, `ReviewTag`s with `source = ai_suggested` and `confirmed_by_author = true` only for tags she kept (Doc 52, Doc 54 §1.2).
- The invitation is a `ReviewRequest` with channel `email` and `in_app`, a single-use link token bound to this appointment and to Sara, `sent_at`, and `expires_at` = 7 days later. Opening the link needs a one-time code, so a forwarded link is not enough. The appointment was confirmed and took place, so the review takes **Path 1** (visit-based) and needs no approval from the shop or from CarPal (MVP Module F).
- AI checks in the form (private info, abusive content) are suggestions to her; moderation decisions are made by rules and humans (MVP §7.5D).
- Showcase consent is a `ConsentRecord` of type `showcase_publishing` scoped to the `PortfolioItem`. Without it the showcase cannot be published (WP12 acceptance).
- Events: `service_record.created`, `review.created` → fraud checks and moderation → `review.published` → provider notification, trust scoring, skill heatmap update.

**Other paths**
- *Bad experience:* she can leave a low rating; the same verified label applies. She can also report the shop for unsafe work, which opens a moderation case.
- *Review without an appointment* (a walk-in, or after the 7-day window): allowed, but it follows **Path 2**. It stays hidden and shows "Waiting for approval" to Sara until the business confirms she was its customer (or does not answer for 7 days) and a CarPal reviewer approves it. It is then published as "Approved by the business and CarPal" and counts at a lower weight (Module F). Chapter 11 shows an example.
- *She never answers the invitation:* reminders stop after the configured limit, and the link expires 7 days after it was sent. Her service record stays verified either way. If she writes the review later from the profile, it follows Path 2, with the appointment attached as evidence.
- *She withdraws showcase consent later:* the showcase is unpublished (Chapter 10).

## Chapter 10 — Afterwards

CarPal becomes Silver's long-term record.

- **Maintenance.** At 160,000 km the app suggests a brake fluid check and reminds her about the left-side control arm. She books both with Reza, this time straight from her favourites.
- **Discover.** Her feed shows new showcases from Reza and nearby shops, and E90 tips. She asks a question in Q&A about winter tyres; a tyre specialist answers.
- **Privacy.** A year later she sells Silver. Before handing it over, she exports Silver's full service history as a file to give the buyer. She marks the car as **sold**. She then withdraws her showcase consent; the before/after post comes down from Reza's portfolio, while the evidence of the completed job stays in the scoring as an appointment.

**Behind the scenes**
- Maintenance suggestions via `suggest_maintenance_checks` (MVP §7.2B) are `AiInsight`s and never absolute claims.
- Discover feed is focused, not a general social feed (MVP Module J). Q&A is a Should-have.
- Data export through a `DataSubjectRequest` (WP2). Withdrawal of `showcase_publishing` consent takes effect immediately (WP2 acceptance).

## Chapter 11 — Looking around

This chapter is not part of the clunk story. It shows how Sara uses CarPal when she already knows what she needs, or just wants to see what is around. It can happen at any time after Chapter 2.

In winter the Corolla needs tyres, and its battery has been slow to start. At lunch near her office she opens **Discover**, picks the Corolla in the vehicle chip, and types in Persian: *"تعویض لاستیک"* (tyre change). She does not open Help Me. Under the search box the app shows what it understood: **Wheels & tyres · Toyota Corolla**, as chips she can edit. The results are tyre shops with evidence for her car.

She tries the other problem in her own words: *"باتری ضعیف شده"* (the battery has got weak). The results are battery shops and a battery seller. A quiet line below the box says *Not sure what's wrong? Describe it in Help Me.* It is only a suggestion; the results are already there.

Then she taps **Browse on map**. The map opens around her office with the **For Corolla** filter on: nine pins. Shops and parts sellers have different icons. A battery seller's pin reads "2 batteries fit your Corolla". She wants more choice, so she turns the filter off with **Show all nearby**. Then she opens **Advanced search** and sets *Open now* and *Rating 4+*. The pins that match stay large; the others shrink to outlines, but she can still tap them.

She taps a tyre shop's pin. The card shows its name, about how far it is, its rating for tyres, a badge, one line on why it matches, and *Open now*. The main button is **Request appointment**. **Directions** is next to it. She taps Directions, and a sheet lists the map apps on her phone. She picks the one she usually uses; the app opens with the shop as the destination, and CarPal remembers her choice. CarPal itself does not show a route.

For the tyres she sends a request from the card. The draft has no triage summary, so she types one line and picks *Wheels & tyres*. The shop sees her description and "Triage: not provided". For the battery she opens the seller's card, taps **Ask about part**, and the seller reserves one for her. Both complete normally, and both give her the usual verified path to a review.

A week later she follows Directions to a small tyre shop she never booked with, and just walks in. There is no appointment. She is happy with the work. The owner logs the job in the provider portal and sends her an invitation. The email has a personal link that works for 7 days. She opens it, confirms with a code, and writes the review. Because the shop sent the invitation, the business has already confirmed her. The review waits for a CarPal reviewer. When it is approved, it appears as **Approved by the business and CarPal**.

**Behind the scenes**
- Screens: S-SHARED-01 Search Entry → C-SEARCH-01 Search Results, or "Browse on map" → S-SHARED-02 Map View (with S-SHARED-03 Filter Sheet) → pin card → S-SHARED-10 Directions Sheet / C-PROV-01 / C-HELP-07 (request draft) / C-INV-03 (stock inquiry) → C-REV-01.
- Search text is normalized (letter forms, digits, spacing) and interpreted into a structured intent. The chips are that intent in a form she can edit. This is rule-based (taxonomy and synonyms) and works with AI off. `MatchSession` is stored with `source = search` and the intent. AI personalization is not needed.
- The map uses tiles from CarPal's own servers (OpenStreetMap data). Pins carry public fields only. A seller is one pin even if many parts match. The "For Corolla" filter and the highlight both use the same match data. No featured pins are shown.
- `Appointment.source = direct_search`. The `PreVisitReport` has triage "not provided" and shows her own description.
- Reviews: the tyre and battery flows are confirmed visits. The walk-in review is **Path 2**: `Review` status `pending_approval`, business confirmation recorded as given (the business sent the link), then a social reviewer's approval, then `approved_without_visit` with a lower weight (Doc 54).
- Events: `search.started`, `map.opened`, `map.pin_selected`, `map.directions_tapped` (with the app chosen), `appointment.requested`, `review.invitation.sent`, `review.approval.requested`, `review.approved`.

**Other paths**
- *Location permission denied:* the map opens on a city she chooses. Nothing is blocked.
- *The vehicle filter leaves fewer than 3 pins:* the map says so and offers **Show all nearby**.
- *Safety words in the search* (for example "brake pedal soft"): a red safety banner appears above the results, with a **Find urgent help** button. The results stay visible.
- *No results:* the app shows "Did you mean…" with spelling and synonym suggestions, and offers to widen the area.
- *The map cannot load:* the screen falls back to the list.
- *The shop is a mobile mechanic:* the map shows its service radius as a circle, not a pin on a street.

## Open points found while writing this story

These are gaps or conflicts in the current docs. They belong to WP0.

1. **Help Me screen IDs conflict.** Doc 90 defines C-HELP-01 to C-HELP-04 (Landing, AI Triage Conversation, Recommendations, Appointment Draft). Doc 55 defines C-HELP-01 to C-HELP-08 with different names. This story uses Doc 55, and so does Doc 21. Doc 90 still has to be updated.
2. **Invoice uploaded by the owner alone.** Doc 52 allows `invoice_verified` when an invoice exists and consent allows it, but does not say who checks the invoice. If any upload counts as verified, Chapter 4's old brake job could be "verified" without any check.
3. **Owner confirmation of provider records.** `ProviderJobLog` has `customer_confirmed`, but `VehicleServiceRecord.verification_status` has no customer-confirmed value. Should the owner be able to confirm or dispute a provider-created record from C-GARAGE-04?
4. **Review window.** Resolved: the invitation link and the in-app window close 7 days after the invitation is sent. The number of reminders (0–3) is set in the admin rules (A-NOTIF-01).
5. **Showcase consent request.** Resolved: C-REV-03 (mobile) and W-REV-02 (web).
6. **Cost range in status notes.** Sara receives an estimated cost while the car is "awaiting parts". The appointment model has no structured estimate field; today it would be free text in a note.
7. **Weight of approval-based reviews.** Path 2 reviews count at a lower weight than visit-based reviews. The number belongs in the scoring protocol (Doc 54).
8. **Trusted users.** After the MVP, users with more than 100 helpful votes who accept an invitation link can become trusted users and act as social reviewers. The rules for their queue are not designed yet.
9. **Email for invitations.** Users who signed up with a phone only have no email. The point at which the app asks for one (Chapter 1 other paths) should be confirmed.
